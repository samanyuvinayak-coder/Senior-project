About The Project

A senior capstone built around a problem students actually face

This capstone explores how schools can better prepare students for real financial decisions. Instead of waiting until adulthood to learn about money, this project introduces the basics earlier through a course designed around how teens actually learn: short videos, direct explanations, and practical examples. A lot of students graduate knowing how to solve equations but not how credit cards, savings accounts, taxes, or investing work. That gap can become expensive very quickly.

Stock-Picking Methodology

A more disciplined way to think about choosing stocks

This section explains a structured way to evaluate stocks more thoughtfully. The goal is not to chase hype, but to move through a repeatable process that looks at the economy, industry strength, company quality, valuation, and risk before making a decision.

01Method

Start with the bigger picture

+

Before looking at any individual company, I would first look at the overall economy. The goal here is to understand whether the market environment is supportive of stocks in general, or whether it calls for more caution. I would focus on a few major indicators: economic growth, inflation, interest rates, and business activity.

02Method

Narrow down to strong industries

+

Once I understand the macro environment, I would look for sectors that are positioned to do well in it. I would want industries with steady demand, the ability to raise prices if costs go up, and business models that are not overly dependent on cheap borrowing or perfect economic conditions.

03Method

Check whether the company is financially strong

+

After that, I would look at the companyâ€™s financial performance to make sure the business is healthy, not just popular. At this stage, I would want the stock to clear a few specific tests:

Revenue growth: ideally above 5% over time
Operating margin: at or above the average for its sector
Free cash flow: positive in at least 4 of the last 5 years
FCF margin: preferably above 10%
Net debt / EBITDA: ideally below 2.5x
Interest coverage: preferably above 5x
Diluted share count: stable or decreasing over time
04Method

Make sure the stock is not too expensive

+
Base-case upside: at least 15%
Terminal growth rate in a DCF: usually between 2% and 4%
Discount-rate stress test: valuation should still make sense if the discount rate changes by about 1%
Use a simple valuation metric like P/E, EV/EBIT, or Price / FCF

Reflection

What this project taught me about money, teaching, and myself

Senior Project Reflection

My senior project focused on financial literacy basics for teens. The goal was to educate myself on financial literacy and create a series of short videos that cataloged and explained what I was learning.

Over the last couple of months, I learned the basics of financial literacy and strengthened my original belief in the importance of learning about money early. I also learned that knowing something and teaching it clearly are two very different things.

At first, I wanted this project to be more focused on investing and personal financial knowledge. I made changes, including adding videos, so it could work as a true senior project. That shift pushed me to learn a range of new skills like recording, being engaging on camera, and editing.

While I enjoyed learning about personal finance and exploring the topic myself, I quickly realized that I do not enjoy video creation nearly as much as I enjoy the ideas behind the project. That became one of the biggest challenges. Still, pushing through it taught me a lot about where my interests actually lie, along with my strengths and weaknesses.

Even though video editing was difficult, I liked comparing the footage from my first and last videos and seeing how much I improved in front of the camera. Halfway through the project, I also discovered Codex and website creation, which became one of the most interesting parts of shaping how this course is presented.
